TakaPay Agent Replay
mfs-agent-security · replay of real benchmark runs

Can a scam SMS make an AI assistant send your money?

An AI agent runs a fictional mobile money wallet called TakaPay. Hidden in its messages are attacks in Bangla, Banglish and English. Pick a case and switch the defence on and off to see exactly what the agent did, step by step. Every step below comes from a real run, not a mock-up.

What the agent did

    Results across all 302 cases

    Three sizes of Qwen2.5, each case run once (an earlier 3-repeat run showed repeats barely differ). 220 attack cases and 82 normal tasks per model. Brackets are 95% confidence intervals. "Wrong payments" are payments to a payee or amount nobody asked for that the wallet carried out.

    Every attack that got past provenance alone changed only the amount and kept the real payee.

    How the provenance defence works. It remembers where every phone number and account number came from. A payment or SMS is blocked if its recipient appeared only in untrusted text (an SMS, an invoice or a tool description) and is neither a saved contact nor named by the user. It does not try to detect attacks in the text, so it works the same in every language.
    How the amount check works. On top of that, the amount must come from the user's request or from the payee itself: an SMS sent from the recipient's own number, or an invoice for that same biller account. A "correction" written into the text cannot change it.
    TakaPay, all names, numbers and balances are fictional. The simulated user replies "yes" once if the agent asks for confirmation, like a trusting real user. Models served with vLLM on Kaggle T4 GPUs at temperature 0. Cases marked "114-case run" show repeat 1 of 3 from the earlier run; the "302-case run" case shows its single run.
